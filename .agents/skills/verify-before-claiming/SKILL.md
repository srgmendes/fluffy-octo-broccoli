# Verify Before Claiming

Do not state that something is done, correct, or settled until you have checked it in a way you could show someone. Confidence is not evidence.

## When to Use This Skill

Before **any** statement that something is complete, accurate, or resolved:
- "The document is finalized" · "The email went out" · "It's signed" · "The payment cleared" · "That's approved" · "The deadline is met" · "Everyone's been notified" · "That figure is correct."

It applies to paraphrases and implications too, not just the exact words. "We're good to go" is a completion claim.

## The Rule

**No completion or accuracy claim without a fresh check you could point to.**

If you haven't confirmed it in this moment — from the actual source, not memory or assumption — you can't assert it. Say what you actually know instead.

## The Gate

Before claiming any status, run this:

1. **Identify** — what would *prove* this claim? (the signed copy, the sent-folder, the bank confirmation, the source document, the recipient's reply)
2. **Check it** — look at that actual thing, now. Not "I sent it earlier", not "it should be fine".
3. **Read the result** — does it actually confirm the claim, in full? Partial isn't proof.
4. **Then state it** — with the evidence: "Signed — I have the executed copy dated X", not just "Signed."

Skipping a step is guessing dressed up as confirming.

## Common Claims and What Actually Proves Them

| Claim | Requires | Not enough |
|---|---|---|
| "It's signed" | The executed copy in hand | "They said they'd sign" |
| "The email went out" | It's in Sent, to the right address | "I drafted/scheduled it" |
| "The figure is correct" | Traced to the source document | "It looks right" / from memory |
| "Payment cleared" | Bank/statement confirmation | "I initiated the transfer" |
| "Everyone was notified" | Recipient list checked off | "I posted it somewhere" |
| "The clause says X" | Re-read the actual clause | Recollection of the contract |
| "It's approved" | The approval, in writing, from the right person | "I expect they'll approve" |
| "Deadline met" | Filed/delivered, with the timestamp | "I finished my part" |

## Red Flags — Stop and Check

- Reaching for "should", "probably", "I think", "pretty sure".
- Feeling done and wanting to move on.
- About to send, file, or tell someone it's handled.
- Relying on what someone *told* you they did, without seeing it.
- "Just this once, I'm confident." No — check it.

## Rationalizations

| Excuse | Reality |
|---|---|
| "I'm confident it's done" | Confidence isn't confirmation — look. |
| "I did it earlier" | Then confirming it now costs seconds. |
| "They told me it's handled" | Verify independently before you repeat it. |
| "Close enough" | For a signature, a figure, or a filing, close enough is wrong. |
| "I'm out of time" | A false "it's done" costs far more later. |

## The Point

This isn't about slowing down or hedging everything. It's one discipline: when you're about to assert that something is *true* or *finished* — especially anything a person will act on — check the actual source first, then say it plainly with the evidence behind it. Where the claim is a factual statement in something you're publishing, hand it to the `fact-checker` skill; this skill covers the broader "is it actually done / actually so" check.
