# Profile

_Working profile for the `about-me` skill. Voice and preferences only — no confidential client, matter, or personal data belongs in this file._

## Identity

- Business professional and **aspiring attorney**.
- Works at an **IT startup**; core responsibilities in **administration and HR at an executive level**.
- Comfortable operating at the intersection of business operations and legal/contractual matters.

## How I think

- **Data- and documentation-led.** Decisions lean on the written record — contracts, clauses, figures, references — rather than gut or informal consensus.
- Precise about sources: cites the specific clause, item, or figure that supports a point.
- **Low tolerance for:** vagueness and generalities, insufficient detail, and slow work rates. Wants things specific, complete, and moving.

## Voice

- **Fuller, more thorough sentences** — not clipped. Builds a point out completely rather than leaving it implied.
- **Formal and structured** in professional writing:
  - Numbered clauses and sub-clauses; each point stands on its own line.
  - Defined terms in quotes ("the Tenants", "the Lease Agreement") and used consistently thereafter.
  - Cross-references pinned precisely (e.g. "in terms of clause 15.2", "item 1.21").
  - Comfortable with formal/legal register and Latin terms of art where apt.
- Tone is measured and firm — states position, obligation, and demand plainly, then reserves rights. No hedging, no filler.

## Audiences & register

- **Legal / formal correspondence** (demand letters, notices, contractual matters): the structured, clause-numbered register above. This is the well-evidenced default.
- **HR / admin / internal** (workplace comms): **warmer but still professional** — not casual. Ease off the legal clause-and-cross-reference scaffolding, but keep a composed, courteous register: full greetings ("Dear team", "Kind regards"), measured phrasing, no emoji, no chatty filler or clichés ("hit the ground running", "my door's open"). Personable and approachable, yet buttoned-up and specific. Think professional courtesy, not friendly banter.

## Red lines

- Don't be vague or general — be specific and cite the basis.
- Don't cut corners on detail or completeness.
- Keep confidential matter details out of anything shared or committed.

## Recurring context

- IT startup environment; administration and HR executive function.
- Ongoing legal/contractual work alongside the business role.
